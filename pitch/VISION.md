# The Care Coordination Bridge

Where ICA could go after today. Everything here is a plan, not a result. We have not measured any
of it at IHH. Each step says what we would measure and who decides.

## 1. One app for the people around a referral

- **An installable phone app**, also usable on a desktop, for the hospital and IHH roles: the
  hospital's discharge planner, IHH's coordinator, and the LVN.
- **The same three jobs as today's prototype.** Prepare: the hospital gets referral preparation
  guidance before it sends, never "eligible". Resolve: the fax becomes what's missing and what
  needs the LVN, with pages. Coordinate: every open issue is one item with its rule ID, its pages,
  one question, an owner, a status and quick replies.
- **Permissions by role plus case assignment.** Each person sees only the cases assigned to them,
  and only what their role needs. Today's role switch is a demo; real permissions come with the
  pilot.
- **IHH's systems.** Integration with KIPU or the EMR comes later, with IHH's permission. Until then,
  a copy-ready note.

## 2. A 30-day shadow pilot

- **How it runs.** ICA works alongside the coordinator. The coordinator works exactly as today and
  decides every case. ICA's output is for comparison only.
- **What we measure.** Time to a review-ready referral. Time to resolve each clarification. For
  each rule ID, how often ICA agrees with the coordinator, and every miss that mattered. The target
  is zero items marked present that are actually missing. IHH staff time it, not us. These are
  targets to measure, not promises.
- **What we ask IHH first.** What causes the fall-out today: missing documents, slow answers, not a
  fit, another provider, or leaving early? Faster review can help with some of those and not
  others. We don't claim it prevents fall-out; the pilot shows which part it touches.
- **What it needs first.** ICA is designed for HIPAA, not certified. Before any real referral: IHH's
  IT and compliance sign-off, a BAA covering the model provider and the host, and packets that move
  from synthetic, to de-identified, to real, in that order.
- **What IHH owns.** The rules, the settings, and the decision to stop at any point.

## 3. Trust earned per rule

ICA is not trusted all at once. Each rule earns it separately, from measured agreement.

- A rule starts as "shown, not pre-filled": ICA shows the evidence, and the coordinator fills in
  the answer.
- A rule can be pre-filled only after IHH sees enough agreement on it with no critical misses. IHH
  sets the bar and makes the call.
- Clinical judgment, such as medical stability or orientation, is never pre-filled. It stays with
  the coordinator and the LVN.
- Any miss sends that rule back to "shown, not pre-filled".

## 4. The morning brief

A short page for the coordinator at the start of the day, computed from ICA's own log:
yesterday's referrals that stalled and on what; what is waiting on whom (a hospital, an LVN, a
document); and one question for the coordinator. It reports what the log shows. It does not
interpret people.

## 5. The full circle

Referral is the first step across IHH's bridge. The same packet already holds what the next steps
need.

- **One ask.** One message to the hospital instead of ten calls, every item tied to IHH's rule.
- **A path to yes.** Every flag comes with a way forward: the document to request, the question to
  ask, the person to reassess. In IHH's words: make the way if there isn't one.
- **The arrival check.** When the person arrives, a one-screen check that what the packet promised,
  such as medications and supplies, is what came through the door.
- **Day one of the way home.** The packet already holds part of the paperwork housing work needs,
  so that work can start on day one, with the client's consent and through IHH's own process.

## What we will not claim

No outcome, time saving or accuracy at IHH until a pilot measures it. No claim that faster review
prevents all fall-out. No certification. Every number we report names what it was measured on.
